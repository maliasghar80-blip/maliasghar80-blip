**Post 1** (image x1.png)
Investors don't fund spreadsheets. They fund assumptions they believe.

I've built valuation and startup models (DCF, PE waterfalls, credit models). The same 6 mistakes show up in almost every founder's model.

Here they are, and how to fix each one 🧵

**Post 2** (image x2.png)
1. Growth typed as a %. "Revenue grows 20% a month" isn't a driver, it's a wish.
2. "1% of a $10B market." It tells an investor nothing about how you'll win customer #1.
3. Costs that don't scale. Revenue 10x, support costs flat? Nobody believes the margin.

**Post 3**
4. No cash tab. Profit and cash are different things. Investors want to know the month you run out.
5. Numbers typed inside formulas. =B4*1.2 means the model breaks the moment someone asks "what if?"
6. Year-5 revenue to the dollar. Precision isn't confidence. Round the outer years.

**Post 4** (image x3.png)
The fix for 1 and 2 is the same: build revenue from the bottom up.

Ad spend → visitors → trials → paying customers → MRR.

Every number can be tested, and improving one improves the result.

**Post 5** (image x4.png)
Then show paid CAC, not blended.

Blended CAC counts free organic customers as if your ads won them. It makes you look ~33% cheaper than you are, and investors know it.

**Post 6** (image x5.png)
The question that quietly decides the meeting:

"Which assumption are you least sure about?"

"None" is the wrong answer. Name it, and say how you'll test it.
